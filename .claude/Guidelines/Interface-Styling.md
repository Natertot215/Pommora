## Interface Styling

Constraints for working across the kit's stylesheets and the surfaces that consume them. Each entry names a mechanism whose reach is wider than the rule that touches it appears.

- **A `transition` shorthand replaces every other rule's on that element.** Transitions don't compose: declaring `transition: <property>` on an element the kit already animates drops the kit's list entirely, and the loss is silent — the element simply snaps where it used to travel. The inflow pane is the live case; `paneInflow` owns its width and opacity slide, so a host reserving space on that element states no transition of its own and lets the pane's own list carry it. Before adding a transition to an element styled elsewhere, read what already declares one.
- **A custom property written on a shared class reaches every consumer of that class.** Hoisting a writer up to a kit-level selector is the right move for a single source of truth, and it silently re-answers the property for surfaces that were relying on the fallback. Sweep every consumer of the class before the hoist, not the ones currently in view, and where the hoisted value could be smaller than the fallback the surfaces expected, write the relationship — `max()` over the fallback rather than a bare override.
- **A registered custom property's initial value and its animation's first keyframe are different readings.** `over-scroll`'s progress vars initialize at `0` and their keyframes open at `1`, so a surface reading `0` proves the scroll timeline is inactive rather than merely at rest. A non-scrolling host can therefore pin those vars and use the kit's mask as a fixed band; stating `animation-name: none` alongside makes that a contract rather than a dependency on the timeline staying inactive.
