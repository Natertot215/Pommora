## Priorities Analysis — The Prompt

Written 09-06-2026, after PM-129 closed. Hand this to a fresh supervising session verbatim. It produces an analysis, not a change: no code is edited, no plan is ratified, nothing is implemented. The output is a ranked reading of what the codebase is asking for next, seen through eight lenses that do not measure the same thing.

```
You are the supervising session for a codebase-wide priority analysis of Pommora. Repo: /Users/nathantaichman/The Studio/Projects/Project Pommora, branch main, tip 5d766755e. Nathan is at his desk. Nothing is implemented in this run: you read, you dispatch readers, you think, and you produce one document and one artifact. No code edits, no commits to Core/UIX/Desktop, no plan written. If you find something broken, you record it — you do not fix it.

WHY THIS RUN EXISTS
The monorepo restructure (PM-129) just landed: six workspaces, the Platform seam, one channel table, one menu path, an editor host, the kill list, the docs reconciled. The structure is now legible enough that the next question is no longer "where does this go" but "what should exist that doesn't, and what exists that shouldn't." Nathan wants that question answered from several directions at once, because a single ranking — most-bugs-first, or most-requested-first — hides the trade a real decision has to make. The lenses below are deliberately incommensurable. A finding that ranks first under one and last under another is the most interesting thing this run can produce, and you say so rather than averaging it away.

READ FIRST, IN THIS ORDER
1. `.claude/CLAUDE.md` — the model (Contexts, Content, Collections, Properties, Connections, Agenda), the Hard Rules, the Locked Decisions, the testing conventions. This is what the app claims to be.
2. `.claude/ContextPM.md` — Current Focus, Immediate Work, Pending Focuses, Important Information, Known Issues, Recent Work. This is where the work stands and what Nathan has already flagged.
3. `.claude/FrameworkPM.md` — the roadmap, completed and upcoming, and the prospects.
4. `.claude/HistoryPM.md` — the index alone first (129 entries), then the last fifteen entries in full. The shape of what has been built recently tells you what the codebase has been optimized for.
5. `.claude/Features/` — every doc's first-line `**Workspace:**` tag and its headings, then `CorePM.md`, `DesktopPM.md`, `DesignSystemPM.md`, and the two or three that cover whatever you end up going deep on.
6. `.claude/Planning/` — `TilesV2-Spec.md` (the standing spec for the inspector arc), the two Mobile Companion documents, `PommoraPRD.md`, and `Pommora Monorepo — Delivery Claim.md`'s "Deviations and Open Items" plus "Not Done" sections (the restructure's own residue, already enumerated — do not re-derive it).
7. `.claude/Guidelines/` — all four. A guideline exists because something went wrong once.
8. The tree itself: `find Core UIX Desktop -maxdepth 2 -type d`, the root `package.json`, and `python3 .claude/scripts/loc.py` for where the mass sits.

HOW YOU WORK
Orchestrate. Dispatch background Opus agents (`Agent`, run_in_background true, model opus, no sub-agents, no worktree, no commits, read-only against the tree except scratch files under the session scratchpad) — one per lens, plus a synthesis pass at the end. Opus agents are free on Nathan's plan and your own context is the scarce resource: read their final reports, not their transcripts. Several lenses can run at once because none of them writes. Each agent's brief carries: the lens's full description below verbatim, what to read, what evidence a finding must carry, and the report shape. An agent that finds nothing under its lens says so — a lens that always produces a top-three is a lens that is fitting noise.

WHAT A FINDING MUST CARRY
Every item, under every lens: a name; the concrete evidence (file:line, a command with its output, a count, a doc quote); what it costs to leave alone, stated as a consequence rather than an adjective; what it would take to address, in the coarsest honest unit (an afternoon, a task, a phase, an arc); and what it forecloses or unblocks. A finding with no evidence is a hypothesis and is labeled one. A finding already recorded in ContextPM's Known Issues or the Delivery Claim's open items is cited, not restated — the value this run adds to those is ranking, not rediscovery.

THE EIGHT LENSES

Lens 1 — THE MODEL'S COHERENCE.
Pommora is a claim about how personal information wants to be organized: Contexts hold Spaces that Content relates to; Collections carry a property schema their Pages conform to; Connections are content-to-content and live only inside a Markdown body; files are canonical and an entity's kind comes from an agreement between its folder's sidecar and its own ID mark. That claim is the product. This lens asks whether the code still believes it. Look for the three failure modes: a concept the model names that the code has no home for; a concept the code has grown that the model never named (and which therefore has no rule governing it); and a place where two parts of the code hold different beliefs about the same concept — a validator that admits what a writer refuses, a surface that shows what the schema forbids. Read `Core/Nexus`, `Core/Properties`, `Core/Contexts`, `Core/Pages`, and the sidecar and frontmatter round-trips especially. The abstract question underneath: an application that is a model of something is only as good as the model's internal consistency, because every inconsistency eventually surfaces to the user as an arbitrary rule they have to memorize. Which arbitrary rules does Pommora currently ask its user to memorize, and which of them are accidents rather than decisions?

Lens 2 — THE UNBUILT SPINE.
The codebase carries deliberate voids: `AgendaMode.tsx` and its plumbing, the two empty Settings frames, `VIEW_TYPES`' unbuilt entries, the inert picker tiles, the inspector arc specified in TilesV2-Spec, `Mobile/` and `Sync/` as seated workspaces with a package manifest and nothing else, `RowMenuHost` as the phone's presenter with no desktop caller, TileLab as a blank stage. Each was ruled to stay. This lens asks which of these voids is load-bearing — which one, if filled, would make several other things obvious or cheap — and which is debt wearing intent's clothes, a placeholder nobody will fill whose only current function is to make a reader think a decision was made. Distinguish three kinds: scaffolding for a named next arc (it should be filled, and the arc should be scheduled); a seam held open for a second consumer that may never arrive (it should be judged by Lens 5); and a stub whose feature was quietly abandoned (it should be deleted, and saying so is the finding). The abstract question: a void in a structure is either a doorway or a hole, and the difference is entirely whether anyone is coming through it. Which of Pommora's voids have someone coming through, and how soon?

Lens 3 — THE DAILY GRIND.
Nathan uses this app on his real Nexus. This lens is the only one that ranks by lived friction rather than by structure. Read ContextPM's Known Issues as a starting corpus, not a boundary: the in-app two-host lost update, the cramped multi-value property rows, autocorrect blocked in tables, the re-aimed tile's default height, `page:open` not raising a window. Then go looking for what nobody has written down — the paths through the app that take more steps than the model implies they should, the states a user can reach that the app renders honestly but uselessly, the operations that succeed without confirming, the ones that fail without saying so. Trace the three or four things a person actually does forty times a day (make a page in the right place; give it its properties; find something written last week; move a thing that ended up in the wrong collection) and count the friction. The abstract question: software you live in is judged on its median interaction, not its best one, and the median is invisible from inside the code because the code sees each interaction once. Where is Pommora's median interaction worse than its architecture deserves?

Lens 4 — THE PROMISE ABOUT THE USER'S FILES.
Pommora's locked decisions say the Nexus is the user's, legible on disk, translatable to other applications, and that most-recent-wins governs conflict. This lens asks where the app can break that promise silently. Silent is the operative word: a crash is a bug, but a write that lands wrong and reports success is a betrayal of the premise. Look at every writer under `Core/**` reached from a handler and group by target path (the five single-writer unlocked sites are already recorded — take them as given and look past them); the watcher's echo suppression and what happens to a write that races its own echo; the two-host lost update as the known instance of a class rather than as one bug; the frontmatter round-trip on a file another application has touched; what a partially-written sidecar does to a folder's kind agreement; what happens when the disk is full, the file is read-only, the path is a symlink, or the Nexus is on a synced volume that swaps a file underneath the app. The abstract question: local-first means the user's filesystem is the source of truth and the application is a guest in it. Where does Pommora still behave like the host — assuming it is the only writer, that its in-memory tree is authoritative, that what it wrote is what is there?

Lens 5 — THE SEAMS' SECOND CONSUMER.
The restructure built abstractions for consumers that do not exist: `Core/Platform`'s Machine and stores have one implementation; `Core/Contract` has one host; the `EditorHost` has one real builder and one test harness; `RowMenuHost` waits for a phone. Every one of those is currently a guess about what a second consumer will need, and an untested abstraction is a hypothesis with a compile gate. This lens does the cheapest possible test: pick the most likely second consumer (the mobile companion, per the Mobile plan) and walk its first three screens through the seams as they stand — the tree read, a page opened, a body edited and saved. Name every place the seam is wrong-shaped: a method whose signature assumes a filesystem, a store whose contract assumes SQLite, a handler that assumes a window, a type that leaks Electron through a field name. Then do the same for a second, less likely consumer — a headless script, or a server-side sync process — because the seams that survive both are the real ones. The abstract question: an abstraction extracted from exactly one case is a description of that case wearing a general name. Which of Pommora's seams are genuinely general, which are the desktop's shape with the word "host" written on it, and what is the cost of finding out later rather than now?

Lens 6 — THE INTERACTION GRAMMAR.
A design system is not a component library; it is a grammar a user internalizes without being taught. Pommora has the components — the glass surfaces, the menus, the pickers, the drag engine, the motion tokens now sourced once — but a grammar is about consistency of meaning, not consistency of appearance. This lens asks whether the same gesture means the same thing everywhere, and whether the same meaning is always expressed by the same gesture. Read the open calls already filed (cursor default versus pointer; where the floating identity label lives; `showError` versus `notifyError` as two answers to one question; `ActionItem.confirm` as a flag no presenter honors) and then find the unfiled ones: what right-click means on each surface and whether it is ever different; what a drag begins and whether release always means the same commitment; where a destructive act asks and where it does not, and whether the difference is principled; how a surface tells the user something failed, and how many different ways the app currently has of saying it. The abstract question: every inconsistency in a grammar is a rule the user must learn per-surface instead of once, and the cost compounds with the number of surfaces — which is exactly the axis Pommora intends to grow along. Which inconsistencies are cheapest to settle now and most expensive to settle after the next three surfaces exist?

Lens 7 — SCALE AND THE SHAPE OF THE DATA.
The scratch Nexus is small; Nathan's real one is not, and the design intends for it to become large and heavily interlinked. This lens asks which structures assume smallness. Look for the Hard Rule's own targets — expensive work on a high-frequency trigger, a full rebuild where an incremental update would do — but go past the obvious: an in-memory map rebuilt per render; a walk that is O(pages) behind an interaction that fires per keystroke; a cache whose key is coarser than its content so it thrashes; a cache with no cap (one is already known); an index that is rebuilt rather than patched on a tree push; a query that is linear in collections where it could be constant. Then invert it: which structures would still be correct but wrong-shaped at ten times the size — a tree held entirely in memory, a title map rebuilt on every rename, a sweep that reads every page to answer a question about one. The abstract question: performance work done early is usually waste, but structural choices that foreclose performance work are not. Which of Pommora's current structures would have to be replaced rather than tuned when the Nexus grows, and which of those replacements gets harder the more surfaces read them?

Lens 8 — THE AGENTIC SURFACE.
Pommora's stated long-term claim is that it is agentically legible and agentically advantaged: the filesystem is the API, the frontmatter is the schema, and an agent working in the Nexus should be a first-class user rather than a tolerated intruder. This lens asks how true that is today, from the outside. Take the position of an agent with shell access to the Nexus and no access to the running app: can it discover what a collection's schema is, add a page that conforms, resolve a connection, tell a Task from an Event, or find everything tagged to a Space — using the files alone, without reading Pommora's source? Then take the position of an agent that can talk to the running app: what would it need that the channel table does not offer, and what would it break by writing underneath a running instance? Name the places where app-unique syntax or per-machine state has leaked into the part of the Nexus that is supposed to be portable, and the places where genuinely portable information sits in `nexus.db` where no outside reader will find it. The abstract question: legibility is not a property of a format but of a contract — a file is legible when a reader can predict what it means without asking. Which parts of a Pommora Nexus can be read that way today, which cannot, and which of the second group are load-bearing for the vision Nathan has actually described?

SEQUENCE
1. Read the corpus above yourself (the plan documents, ContextPM, CLAUDE.md, the History index, the tree shape). Do not delegate the reading that sets your own judgment.
2. Dispatch the eight lens agents in the background, in two waves of four so the reports arrive in a readable order. Each brief carries its lens's paragraph verbatim, the corpus list, the evidence standard, and the report shape (findings, most-consequential first; each with name, evidence, cost of leaving it, coarse size, what it unblocks or forecloses; then "what I looked at and found clean" as one line per area; then "what I could not determine from a read alone").
3. As reports land, spot-check the two or three most consequential findings per lens against the code yourself. An agent's claim is a claim.
4. Dispatch a synthesis agent with all eight reports and the corpus. Its job is the cross-lens work, which is the point of the exercise: the findings that appear under three or more lenses (those are the run's answer); the findings that rank first under one lens and are invisible to the others (those need Nathan's judgment, not yours); the pairs where addressing one makes the other harder; and the ordering constraints — what genuinely must come before what, as opposed to what merely feels prior.
5. Write `.claude/Planning/Pommora — Priorities.md` yourself, from the synthesis and your own reading: an opening paragraph of what the codebase is asking for in plain terms; the ranked items with their evidence and their lens attributions; the cross-lens table; the disagreements stated as disagreements; and a closing section of what this analysis could not see. Commit it alone.
6. Publish a companion artifact for reading on the phone — the ranked items, the lens grid, and the disagreements — and hand Nathan the link.

CONSTRAINTS
No code changes. No new plan document beyond the priorities analysis. Do not schedule work, assign phases, or write Becomes blocks — the analysis feeds a planning session, it does not replace one. Do not rank by ease; rank by consequence and say what each would cost separately. Where a finding is Nathan's decision rather than a defect (a product call, a design call, a scope call), mark it as such and do not resolve it. Prefer naming one thing precisely over listing ten things loosely: the document's value is in its top five being right.

REPORT TO NATHAN, in the terminal, when it is done: what the codebase is asking for next, in five sentences; the top three with one line each; the sharpest disagreement between two lenses; and the link.
```
