## Trash Recovery — Decision Log

### Frame

- **Purpose:** Give the deletion record its missing half. The `.trash` engine — bundles, records, resolution, restore — ships and is tested end to end with no caller. This session specs the surface that reads it and the two actions that spend it.
- **Core Value:** A deleted page can be found and put back without leaving the app or touching Finder.
- **Success Criteria:** A page deleted in Pommora appears in a browsable list with its title, its former location, and when it went; restoring it returns it to the tree and to reach immediately, without a nexus reload; permanently deleting it hands the file to the operating system's trash and drops the row.

### Decisions

#### A — Placement & Naming

- **A-1:** [confirmed] The trash is a **leaf on the Settings window's category rail**, bottom-anchored under a divider. `.settings-rail-list` is `flex: 1` and is currently the rail's only child, so a sibling appended after it sinks to the bottom for free — the same mechanism `.navwindow-style-toggle` uses (`navWindow.css:158`). No `margin-top: auto` needed; it does need `z-index` above the resize corners so its own corner stays clickable.
- **A-2:** [confirmed] The rail's leaf registry must grow a **render seam**. Every leaf today is a list of personalization toggles (`TOGGLES: Record<CategoryKey, Toggle[]>`); Trash is the first leaf with a body of its own. The showcase's leaf registry (`design-system/showcase/leaves/registry.tsx` — the `Leaf` type at :16, `LEAVES` at :32) already models a per-leaf render and is the shape to follow.
- **A-3:** [confirmed] `SettingsWindow` → **`NexusSettings`**. The word is genuinely shared: an unrelated `Components/Detail/SettingsPane.tsx` is the per-view config dropdown, and while the component itself has only three importers, its stylesheet is imported by twenty modules — so "settings" reads as two different things across a wide slice of the renderer. The rename touches **seven locations** — two filenames, the exported component, its inner body component, the CSS import, and both the import and the mount in `App.tsx`. Nothing else; there is no route string, store key, or type union carrying the name.
- **A-4:** [confirmed] The leaf wears Lucide **`trash`**, already in the curated registry.
- **A-5:** [assumed] **The rename stops at the component and its files.** The CSS class namespace, the geometry-stash `id`, and the rail's `windowId` stay as they are — internal strings with no user-facing effect, whose churn would touch more than the ambiguity being fixed.

#### B — Window Chrome

- **B-1:** [confirmed] The Settings window's default size becomes **850 × 600**, matching NavWindow **by choice**. NavWindow passes no `bounds` and inherits `PreviewPane`'s shared default; Settings keeps its own explicit `WIN` constant with the same numbers, so the two stay independently tunable. Its `minW`/`minH` floors are a separate question from its opening size.
- **B-2:** [confirmed] The rail's `padding-top: 26px` is **removed**, not filled. The band exists to clear a lead-cluster glyph the window never renders — `PreviewPane` only draws it when an `onScan` prop is passed, and Settings passes none. The `×` the comment blames sits top-*right*, over the body, not over the rail.
- **B-3:** [assumed] **The body's `padding-top` stays.** It clears the `×`, which does sit above it — the same comment was wrong about the rail only because the rail is on the other side of the window.

- **B-4:** [confirmed] **The window's floor rises with its default.** Matching NavWindow's *default* alone would have lowered the floor rather than raised it — NavWindow inherits a smaller minimum than Settings declares — so both numbers move together, and the floor lands where a title, a breadcrumb and the date lane still fit. The exact value is a single constant and a knob.
- **B-5:** [confirmed] **The trash body is not a window-drag surface.** Four classes are named as drag surfaces today and the match is on the element itself, so blank space in a settings panel moves the window; the trash leaf keeps its own class out of that list, leaving its empty space free to mean something.
- **B-6:** [assumed] **A custom leaf body owns its own layout.** `PreviewPane` renders `children` bare as a flex child of `.ppane-row` — no wrapper, no padding, no scroller — so the body supplies its own `flex`/`min-*` sizing, its own top inset clearing the floating `×`, and its own `--edge-fade` knob, which is registered non-inheriting and never cascades in. The showcase's leaves already work this way: the host brings nothing and the leaf brings its layout.
- **B-7:** [assumed] **The leaf registry holds a component, not a `render()` thunk.** The showcase calls `leaf.render()` as a plain function, which puts any hooks inside it in the *host's* identity and lets two leaves of one type share a position's state. A settings registry storing a component type gets a real reconciliation boundary for free, which a leaf holding fetched rows and selection needs.
- **B-8:** [assumed] **The leaf entry becomes a discriminated union.** `CategoryKey` derives from `CATEGORIES as const` and `TOGGLES` is a total `Record<CategoryKey, Toggle[]>`, so a category cannot exist today without a toggle array. A union — a toggle list or a body — is the smaller change and matches the existing invitation in `LingerRow`'s own comment, which says the schema generalizes once a second non-toggle shape exists.

#### C — The Data Channel

- **C-1:** [confirmed] `listBundles` reaches the renderer through **one new enveloped bridge channel**, on the house recipe: a `bridge.ts` entry, a preload dialer, a `serveBridge` registration under `kind: 'envelope'`, and the handler module — each end a compile error without the others.
- **C-8:** [confirmed] **`listBundles` keeps the artifact name it already looks up.** It computes the artifact to decide whether a deletion finished and then discards it, while the row's title has no other source — re-reading every bundle directory to recover a name the walk just held would be a second enumeration answering a question the first already answered.
- **C-2:** [confirmed] **The deleted date is parsed from the bundle's folder name.** No record variant carries a timestamp; the only record of when a deletion happened is the ISO stamp `trashStamp()` writes into the bundle leaf (`<stamp>__[<n>__]<basename>.deleted`). The stamp is `:` and `.` replaced with `-`, so parsing it back is a fixed unreplace, not a guess. Folder mtime is the alternative and is worse — a sync, a restore-from-backup, or a hand-move rewrites it.
- **C-3:** [assumed] The channel returns a **shaped row, not the raw `ListedBundle`**. Main owns the parse — bundle path → `{ bundlePath, title, formerPath, deletedAt }` — so the renderer never reasons about `.deleted` suffixes, stamp encoding, or the six-way record union. Path formatting reuses `pagePathText`.
- **C-4:** [assumed] The list is **fetched on the leaf opening and refetched after every action it takes**. `.trash` is excluded from the watcher by design (`watcher.ts:38-51`), so no push will ever arrive; there is no live-update path and none is wanted. A deletion performed elsewhere in the app while the leaf is open leaves the list stale until it is reopened.
- **C-5:** [confirmed] The list shows **every artifact-bearing kind** — `page` · `collection` · `set` · `space` · `context` — and excludes only `property`, whose bundle holds a record and no artifact. A pages-only list would have hidden the second-most-likely way work is lost: a deleted Collection or Set takes its pages with it *inside its own bundle*, so those pages never become bundles of their own and would never have appeared. The restore engine is already kind-agnostic and tested across all five.
- **C-6:** [confirmed] A row wears **the nexus's default glyph for its kind** — `entityIcon(kind, undefined, defaults)`. The entity's own stored icon is never read, so what a row *is* can't be mistaken for what it was decorated with; the nexus-wide per-kind override still applies, since it is per-kind and stays unambiguous. This is the affordance the function was built with: its contract states a surface passes `own: undefined` to mean "type-level glyph."
- **C-7:** [confirmed] **Context becomes a kind that carries its own default icon, and the two glyphs separate.** Context takes the grid; Space moves to `layout-dashboard`. This resolves the collision at its source rather than in the trash list: a Context had no default of its own, so the sidebar drew one with the Space kind's glyph, and any surface showing both kinds inherited that ambiguity. → L.

#### D — Actions

- **D-1:** [confirmed] **Restore** is the existing `{ op: 'restore', bundlePath }` through the existing `mutate` channel. Routing it through the store's `mutate` gives the tree refetch for free — `restore` has no optimistic-patch case, so it falls through to the unconditional `load()`. The page's `PageID` stands: nothing is re-minted.
- **D-2:** [confirmed] Restore **does not open or focus** the restored page. It leaves the trash list and the tree gains it; nothing else moves.
- **D-3:** [confirmed] Permanent delete needs a **new main-side op**. It cannot reuse `delete`: `isReserved` (`mutate.ts:123`) explicitly refuses every path under `.trash`, and that guard is deliberate defense against a hostile renderer. The new op takes a `bundlePath`, resolves it under the root, asserts it *is* under `.trash`, and hands it to the already-wired `trashToSystem`.
- **D-4:** [confirmed] **The artifact alone leaves, and the spent bundle is removed behind it.** A user who empties a page from Pommora's trash and later opens the system's should find `Note.md`, not a stamped `.deleted` folder wrapping it beside a record JSON. The record has no value past the moment its entity is given up for good.
- **D-16:** [confirmed] **Main reads the switch itself, per operation, and the renderer never carries it.** A renderer-supplied flag choosing between *recoverable* and *gone forever* is the most dangerous message this feature could send, and the write path has no route to personalization today — it reads app config, never the nexus settings. The precedent is a targeted single-key settings read that deliberately avoids a full walk; a sibling of it folded into the write path's dependencies gets per-operation freshness for the same handful of lines.
- **D-15:** [confirmed] **The erase path already has its precedent and its guard.** Restore spends a bundle with a recursive force-remove after a trash-prefix and suffix assertion; the emptying action is the same shape with the artifact's destination swapped by the switch. Nothing new is invented for the file operation itself.
- **D-9:** [confirmed] **Where the artifact goes is a setting, and its default is the system trash.** A new switch — **Permanently Delete Files** — governs the emptying action: off, the artifact is handed to the system trash and the operating system owns the last undo; on, it is erased from the machine outright. The row reads *"Permanently deleted files will be deleted from this computer, keeping this off will move them to system trash."*
- **D-10:** [confirmed] **`trashMode` stays stubbed and unsurfaced.** It keeps its existing shape in the code with no UI and no writer, waiting on a NexusSettings session of its own — where it and the new switch can be presented together as the pair they are, rather than one arriving alone and inviting the confusion.
- **D-11:** [assumed] **The switch lives in per-Nexus personalization.** The per-device scope is where `trashMode` sits and is the better semantic fit for a machine-local filesystem behavior — but it has no renderer path *in either direction*: no setter, no reader, no store slice, no channel. Choosing it means building the entire renderer↔main path for one boolean. Personalization already carries the setter, the write lock, the unknown-key preservation and the clean-file discipline, and the preference being expressed — *I want emptying to mean gone* — is a user's disposition rather than a property of a machine.
- **D-12:** [confirmed] **A new personalization key must be added to the read coercer or it is invisible.** `Personalization` has no zod schema; validation is a hand-written per-key coercer in the read path, and a key absent from it persists to disk and never reaches the renderer. The switch would appear to work, write correctly, and read back as its default forever.
- **D-13:** [confirmed] **The toggle row needs no change.** Its key is typed to the personalization keys and D-11 puts the switch there, so the switch is an ordinary row and the type stays as it is.
- **D-14:** [confirmed] **The hint ships as written.** It is the longest in the panel and wraps to two or three lines; the rows carry no clamp, it renders correctly, and the copy is the author's.
- **D-5:** [confirmed] **The action reads "Delete".** It is true in both configurations — the item leaves the trash either way — and the word *permanently* belongs to the switch that decides what leaving means, not to the action that obeys it.
- **D-6:** [confirmed] **The trash gets its own confirm, and its explanatory line reads the switch.** The ordinary delete's cannot be reused: it is a closure inside the sidebar's menu builder that hardcodes a single title, runs the delete itself on confirm, and promises a destination from the *old* trash mode — which is wrong in both of the new switch's positions. The new one names the system trash or outright erasure according to what will actually happen.
- **D-8:** [confirmed] **A batch permanent delete confirms on its own dialog.** The ordinary delete's asks about one named title, which would let a user confirm a single-item question and lose five things. The batch asks its own, and reports what actually went afterwards.
- **D-7:** [confirmed] **A refusal that survives the destination picker reaches the user as a native dialog; a batch reports refusals as counts.** `resolveRecord` refuses on four grounds — `parent-gone`, `cannot-hold`, `unaddressable`, `id-live` — plus an `exists` fault when something the tree cannot see squats the target. Every refusal already carries user-facing text (`REFUSAL_TEXT`, `provenance.ts:422-427`); what is undecided is where it surfaces, and what a batch does when some items refuse. **`id-live` is the one refusal H makes no promise about** — a living identity may never be written over, and that law is out of scope to touch.

#### H — Restoring Into a Chosen Home

- **H-21:** [confirmed] **The row carries its kind and whether its home still resolves.** The menu is built before any restore is attempted, so it cannot learn either by trying; the listing holds both the record and the tree at the moment it shapes a row, which is the one place the answers are free.
- **H-1:** [confirmed] **A homeless entity's Restore becomes a submenu of destinations.** Where the recorded parent no longer resolves, restore currently refuses and stops; instead the menu offers the live places that kind may legally land, and the pick becomes the destination. The refusal stops being a dead end and becomes a question.
- **H-2:** [confirmed] **Only three kinds can ever be homeless**, which bounds the submenu precisely. A Context has no parent — it always re-enters the registry at `.nexus/contexts`. A Collection's parent is the nexus root, which cannot go missing. So relocation applies to **Page** and **Set** (→ any Collection or Set) and **Space** (→ any Context), and to nothing else.
- **H-3:** [confirmed] **The destination matrix is already law elsewhere and is not re-invented here.** A page or Set may land only in a Collection or a Set — the same admission `mutate.ts` enforces as the one main-side move check — and a Space may land only in a Context. The submenu lists exactly what those rules admit.
- **H-4:** [confirmed] **Placement reuses `resolveRecord` with the parent substituted — and that alone is not enough, because the resolver is not the record's only reader.** Substituting the parent gets sibling disambiguation, the `.md` strip-and-restore, the Space's collision-free title and the live-id refusal for free. It also silently changes what the returning content is *reconciled against*, which is a separate mechanism with its own consequences (→ H-19, H-20).
- **H-19:** [confirmed] **A relocation judges returning content against the destination's schema, and that is the intent.** Values travel with the entity, and whatever the destination's configuration cannot hold is dropped on the way in — the same reconciliation a homecoming gets, pointed at the home the user chose instead of the one it left. This is a deliberate departure from an ordinary move, which preserves values untouched: a move relocates a live entity, while a restore reconciles a frozen one against a world that kept going. `restoreArtifact` derives the scrub's owning Collection from the *resolved* directory and hands it to `scrubReturning`, which drops every governed key nothing live stands behind.
- **H-20:** [confirmed] **Relocating a Space rewrites frontmatter on every page that tagged it, and the result says so.** Membership re-applies against the substituted parent, so a Space restored into a different Context writes that Context's key onto every page that tagged it — one menu pick fanning out to as many page writes as the Space had members, and the reapply's only failure signal today is a console warning.
- **H-5:** [confirmed] **All three "no valid home" refusals get the submenu**, not just `parent-gone`: `unaddressable` (the delete never resolved a parent) and `cannot-hold` (the recorded parent id is alive but is now the wrong kind to hold this) leave the user in the identical position and want the identical remedy. `id-live` does not — a destination cannot fix a duplicate identity.
- **H-6:** [confirmed] **A missing home stops being a verdict and becomes a question.** `parent-gone` and its two siblings never surface as refusal text; the resolver still returns them, and the surface reads them as the signal to ask where. What remains user-facing is only what a destination cannot answer: a live identity, an unseen squatter at the target, an unreadable record, and an unfinished deletion — the last of which `listBundles` already skips, so no row should be able to reach it.
- **H-7:** [confirmed] **Nothing climbs.** A page whose Set is gone is never filed into that Set's Collection, and no entity is ever placed at the nexus root as a fallback — the resolver refuses instead, and refusing is what hands the choice to the user. This is already how the code behaves: the container branch refuses on a missing parent without consulting the chain above it, and the root branch admits Collections alone. The picker replaces the dead end, not the discipline.
- **H-8:** [confirmed] **The destination submenu is nested, and the pattern already exists as the card menu's `Move To ▸`.** `MoveTarget` (`shared/cardMenu.ts`) is a `{ label, path, children? }` node; `buildMoveTargets` (`CardsView.tsx:891`) walks Collections and their Sets recursively; `moveNode` (`main/cardMenu.ts:26-38`) maps it to Electron submenus with the convention a native menu requires — a container repeats its own name as its submenu's first row above a separator, since a parent item can't itself be clicked. The grip menu's `Page Source ▸` is the same shape and adds the empty case: the row renders `enabled: false` rather than vanishing.
- **H-9:** [confirmed] **`buildMoveTargets` is hoisted out of `CardsView` rather than copied.** It is a pure tree walk with no view dependency, currently sitting inside a 1400-line component because it had exactly one caller. A second caller is the moment it moves.
- **H-10:** [assumed] **The node must carry the container's id, not only its path.** `MoveTarget.path` serves `movePage`, which is path-addressed; restore is id-addressed — `resolveRecord` looks its parent up by id, and the whole record model is ids-over-names. Widening the node with the id it already has in the tree keeps one shape serving both callers; deriving the id from the path main-side would reintroduce name-addressing at the one seam built to avoid it.
- **H-11:** [confirmed] **A Space's destination list is the Context registry, flat.** No Context parents another, so there is no tree to walk — the roster is `tree.contexts` in registry order, and the same node shape carries it with no children.
- **H-17:** [confirmed] **A batch calls the mutate channel directly per item and refreshes once at the end.** That action re-walks the entire nexus after every op except the two value-only ones, so restoring five things would pay five full-tree reads — the exact "reload the entire Y" the house rules forbid. Either the renderer calls the mutate channel directly per item and refreshes once at the end, or the batch becomes a single main-side op. The first keeps each restore atomic and independently refusable, and gives up the free error dialog `store.mutate` provides; the second is one round trip and one refresh, and has to invent its own partial-failure reply.
- **H-12:** [assumed] **A batch restores everything that knows where it belongs, and leaves the rest to be asked.** Homeless members keep their rows and are named in the result; each is then answered individually through the picker it already has. No new mechanism is added, because none is needed — a batch is a convenience over the single action, and the single action already handles the case the batch can't.
- **H-13:** [assumed] **One destination can't serve a batch**, which is why the alternative was dropped rather than deferred. Checked rows may be different kinds with incompatible destination matrices — a Space takes a Context, a page takes a container — and even among one kind they came from different homes, so a single pick would file unrelated things together on the strength of one click.
- **H-14:** [confirmed] **A batch reports what it did and what it couldn't** — how many were restored, and how many could not be resolved — so a partial success never reads as a whole one. The all-homeless batch needs no special case: it reports restoring none, which is the honest answer.
- **H-15:** [confirmed] **The report names the kind when it can and generalizes when it cannot.** A batch of one kind reads that kind's plural; a mixed batch reads "items". It is a nice-to-have rather than a gate — the counts are what carry the meaning.
- **H-18:** [assumed] **A single restore keeps riding `store.mutate`.** Its refetch is the mechanism that makes a restored entity reachable without a nexus reload, its failure already reaches the user as a native dialog, and one op paying one walk is the cost the whole write path pays everywhere else.
- **H-16:** [assumed] **The report is a native message box.** The app has no toast, banner or status surface of any kind, and every user-facing outcome today is a `dialog.showMessageBox` — including the delete path's own confirm and its `Couldn't complete that action.` failure notice, whose shape a restore report would follow. Introducing a first in-app notification surface for this feature would be building a second thing.

#### I — Breadcrumbs

- **I-1:** [confirmed] **A row's breadcrumb resolves live from the recorded parent id, not from the frozen `.trash` chain.** The bundle's mirrored path froze at the moment of deletion; the record's `parent` is an id. Resolving the id against the current tree is what makes a breadcrumb tell the truth after a rename, and it is the same governing law the record itself is built on — ids over name-based locations, titles riding along as labels.
- **I-2:** [confirmed] **A Space's breadcrumb names its Context, and a renamed Context is followed correctly.** The Space branch of `resolveRecord` finds its group by `record.parent.id` and builds the destination from `group.def.title` — the *current* title — so a Space deleted from "Projects" and restored after that Context was renamed to "Ventures" lands in Ventures and should read as Ventures beforehand. Context identity is an ordinary minted ULID in `.nexus/contexts.json` and renames are id-keyed, so the id survives what the title doesn't.
- **I-3:** [assumed] **The frozen chain is the fallback, not the source.** When a recorded parent id resolves to nothing, there is no live breadcrumb to draw and the mirrored `.trash` path is the only surviving evidence of where the entity lived. Such a row is exactly the homeless case H covers, so its breadcrumb should read as historical rather than current.

#### E — The Surface

- **E-1:** [confirmed] **The surface is the nav list's styling, not the table's.** Rows carry the `.nav-item` treatment whole — the kind glyph, the title, and the right-aligned `NavCrumbs` breadcrumb — with a deleted-date column added after them. `TableView` was never a candidate on inspection: it takes `{ source: CollectionNode | SetNode }` and resolves its own schema, saved view, values and orders from that node, with no row-array entry point anywhere in its 1955 lines.
- **E-2:** [confirmed] A row therefore reads **glyph · title · breadcrumb · deleted date**. The breadcrumb is the "path" — the nav list already resolves and renders one, and I-1 governs what it resolves against.
- **E-3:** [confirmed] **The deleted date is a real column** — its own fixed track at the window's right edge, cells left-aligned within it, so the dates line up down the list rather than floating after each breadcrumb. The nav list is flexbox and carries no shared geometry, so this track is the one piece of column structure the surface adds.
- **E-9:** [confirmed] **The columns wear heading rows in the house style**, as an embedded table view draws them — an icon leading a label. **File Name** takes the title glyph, **Time Deleted** takes `clock-fading`. The clock joins the curated registry rather than riding the full-Lucide fallback, which exists so a user-chosen icon resolves while app chrome draws from the roster.
- **E-10:** [confirmed] **The glyph sits in the heading, not on every cell.** A column that names itself once at the top does not need to repeat itself down the list.
- **E-15:** [confirmed] **A heading row implies no sorting here, because it never has.** The house heading carries no click handler, no sort indicator and no `aria-sort` anywhere; sorting lives entirely in the view settings pane. What a heading does imply is drag-to-reorder and a right-click menu — neither of which this surface offers either, and neither of which a plain label promises. Rendering a non-interactive heading withholds nothing.
- **E-16:** [confirmed] **The heading reads as bare column labels over the data** — no fill, no bottom seam — which is the reference given and the right weight for a small panel inside a floating window. It cannot *take* the embedded form, though: that form is a descendant rule keyed to the view-embed tile's own body class, with no modifier to opt into. Either those two shed declarations become a real modifier both consumers wear, or the leaf restates them and the app carries the same rule twice.
- **E-20:** [confirmed] **The leaf must declare the token scopes of every surface it borrows, and this is the single most likely way it ships broken.** The table's tunables are deliberately scoped to the table's own surface so they cannot leak, and the nav row's inset is declared only by the two windows that host one. The Settings window is in neither scope, and an unset custom property with no fallback resolves to its initial value in silence — no error, no lint, nothing to notice. The heading would render with no cell padding, black label text and invisible segment dividers; the row checkbox would centre two points *inside* the content padding, overlapping the kind glyph. Both surfaces look correct in the file and wrong on screen.
- **E-17:** [confirmed] **The glyphs come from the one place the app names them.** File Name takes the reserved title glyph, which lives beside the property-type map precisely so every surface draws it from one source. Time Deleted takes `clock-fading`, which joins the curated registry — the registry is the roster, and app chrome never reaches past it into the full icon set.
- **E-18:** [confirmed] **Alignment needs no mechanism beyond what the nav row already has.** The rows stay flexbox and the headings sit left-aligned over the lanes the rows already establish — the title lane starting where titles start, the date lane over a column of fixed width. The table's shared-track grid is not adopted and neither is its filler.
- **E-19:** [confirmed] **The headings always show their icons.** The house table hides column icons behind a per-view toggle that defaults to hidden; this surface has no view and no toggle, and the icons are what name the two columns, so it departs from that default deliberately.
- **E-4:** [confirmed] **The trash scores its own rows with `fuzzyScore` directly.** The scorer is pure; its caller takes an entry carrying a navigation ref, and a trashed entity has nothing to put there. Widening that type to admit a subject that cannot be navigated to would bend the navigation layer around a surface outside it.
- **E-5:** [confirmed] **The trash names the date format it wants.** `formatDate` takes a date and time format that normally arrive from a property's column configuration; a deleted entity has none, so the surface states them once as its own constant rather than inventing a configuration to read them from.
- **E-6:** [assumed] **Rows are newest-first.** `listBundles` returns filesystem order, which is arbitrary; the stamp is already parsed for the date column, so the ordering costs nothing and what was just lost sits at the top.
- **E-7:** [confirmed] **The empty state reads "Trash is empty."**
- **E-8:** [confirmed] **The inline search inputs fold into one component**, and the trash becomes its fourth consumer rather than its fourth copy. The extraction is behavioral only — every surface must still look exactly as it does now, so per-surface appearance stays with the caller and only the shared shape moves.
- **E-11:** [confirmed] **The shared component styles nothing its callers disagree on, and NavWindow's placeholder is the proof.** The three inputs agree on the controlled value, `spellCheck={false}`, and the chrome reset; they disagree on nearly everything else — two are transparent and one is a filled field with a radius and a focus ring, one overrides the type ramp entirely, one autofocuses through a ref, one is wrapped in a divider row and one in nothing, and the wrappers are three genuinely different objects. **NavWindow's placeholder has no colour rule and falls to the browser default**, where the other two are tertiary; a shared component that styles placeholders uniformly changes NavWindow's appearance, which is the one thing this refactor promised not to do.
- **E-12:** [confirmed] **`EntityGlyph` is not reused; the trash resolves its own glyph.** It renders a pre-resolved icon string rather than resolving one — `entityIcon` is called once per node while the tree index is built, and a trashed entity is not in the tree. The trash resolves its own glyph and renders `Icon` directly, which is also what makes C-6's kind-only rule enforceable here rather than inherited.
- **E-13:** [confirmed] **The date is its own column, after the breadcrumb.** It is a lane of its own rather than trailing content inside the row's main flex group, which is what lets the dates line up down the list and gives the heading something to sit over.
- **E-14:** [confirmed] **A checked row wears no fill.** The checkbox alone carries the state; the row keeps the hover treatment the nav row already has and gains nothing, so `--state-selected` stays untouched here.

#### F — Selection

- **F-1:** [confirmed] **There is no multi-select anywhere in the app to inherit from.** `store.ts:250` holds a single discriminated `SelectionState`; no view, menu, or list carries an array or a `Set`. Selection here is local component state, not store state — it belongs to the leaf and dies with it.
- **F-2:** [confirmed] The checkbox is **MarkdownPM's**, wrapped in real control semantics. It is already built to be pressed and already reads as an empty box at rest, which is what this surface wants; the table's checkbox is a value renderer with no role, no handler and no keyboard, and wrapping it would mean adding all of that anyway.
- **F-3:** [confirmed] The gutter checkbox follows **`.nav-item-pin`** (`navList.css:20-40`): absolutely positioned into the row's left inset, `stopPropagation` on pointerdown so the control never arms the row's own gesture.
- **F-4:** [assumed] **Right-clicking an unchecked row acts on that row alone**, whatever else is checked. The checked set is a deliberate construction, and a menu that silently retargets it would spend it on a click that never named it.
- **F-5:** [confirmed] **Select-all ships, on the editor's checkbox rather than the table's.** MarkdownPM's is the interactive one — a pointer cursor, an empty box at rest, an accent-tinted box with a centred glyph when set — where the table's is a bare value visual with no control semantics. Neither carries an indeterminate state today; on this one it is a glyph swap, since the mark is an absolutely-centred SVG inside the box and the registry already holds the dash.
- **F-6:** [assumed] **Checkboxes are always visible**, resting empty rather than appearing on hover. The geometry copies `.nav-item-pin`; the reveal behavior does not.

#### G — The Menu

- **G-1:** [confirmed] A **new model-driven native menu** on the established recipe — a shared model, one bridge channel, one preload dialer, one main-side popper, one `serveBridge` registration under `kind: 'menu'` — popping through **`popReturningMenu`**, not `popModelMenu`. The latter is `menuTemplate` alone, and `menuTemplate` emits a flat label-and-click row with no nesting and no disabled state, so it structurally cannot express H-8's destination submenu. Both menus in the app that carry submenus already reach past it for exactly this reason.
- **G-2:** [confirmed] It **cannot reuse `pageMenu.ts`**. `PageMetaAction` is a closed ten-member union of live-page actions with no `restore` and no permanent delete, and `pageMetaMenuSubset` only filters that list. A trashed page shares none of its actions — it cannot be renamed, previewed, opened, or linked to.
- **G-3:** [confirmed] It is a **returning menu**: main pops, resolves the action back, the renderer performs the write. The alternative — an owning menu running the mutation in place, as `contextMenu.ts` does — would leave the leaf unable to refresh its own list.
- **G-4:** [confirmed] **One row reads Restore · Delete; a checked set reads Restore All · Delete All.** No menu in the app has ever taken N items or rendered a count, and a count in the label would be the first — the plural carries the same meaning without inventing one.

#### J — The Sweep

Findings from the don't-forget matrix, run because the design is both interactive and structural.

- **J-1:** [assumed] **A renderer-supplied destination id is validated main-side, never trusted.** The bundle path already passes `resolveUnderRoot` plus a trash-prefix and suffix assertion; a chosen parent arrives by the same untrusted route and needs the same treatment — it must resolve to a live container of a kind that may hold what is returning. The existing per-kind admission is the check; what is new is remembering that the pick is a message, not a fact.
- **J-2:** [confirmed] **The permanent-delete op repeats the bundle-path guard rather than inheriting it, and a readable record is part of that guard.** `isReserved` refuses `.trash` for every ordinary mutation precisely so a hostile or buggy message cannot reach in; the one op that must reach in makes that assertion itself. Path, root and suffix are not sufficient — `.trash` mirrors the nexus, so a user's own folder named with the bundle suffix becomes a chain directory that passes all three while holding real bundles inside it. A bundle is a folder holding a record, which is the same test the restore path opens with.
- **J-3:** [confirmed] **Rows are a snapshot and stale rows are ordinary, not exceptional.** `.trash` is unwatched, so a list is only as current as its last fetch — a bundle spent and then acted on again by a lingering row is the expected case, not an edge one. It degrades safely: the path resolver fails before the record is ever read, so the user is told the path was not found. That is accurate and unhelpful, and it is the wording a spent row deserves better than.
- **J-4:** [assumed] **The permanent delete's failure mode is an invisible orphan, and the ordering chooses which.** Handing the artifact over and then removing the bundle leaves, if that removal fails, a bundle holding a record and no artifact — indistinguishable from a deletion that never finished, so `listBundles` skips it and the folder lingers unreachable. The reverse order risks the opposite orphan while the artifact is still live. Artifact first is the right trade: the failure leaves litter rather than a file nobody can reach.
- **J-5:** [confirmed] **Clicking a row toggles its checkbox.** A trashed entity cannot be selected or opened, so the click spends itself on the one thing the row does offer, and the whole row becomes the checkbox's target rather than the small box alone.
- **J-6:** [confirmed] **Escape leaves the search before it leaves the window.** With a query in the field it clears the query; with the field already empty it falls through to the window's own dismissal. The pane's Escape listener already stands down on a handled event, so the field claims it rather than the window being taught about the field.
- **J-7:** [assumed] **The trash's read cost grows without bound and is accepted for this iteration.** Nothing in `.trash` is ever pruned, so `listBundles` walks a directory that only ever grows, and it walks all of it every time the leaf opens. At the sizes a personal nexus reaches this is not felt; it is nonetheless the surface's ceiling, and it is the reason pruning sits in Prospects rather than nowhere.
- **J-8:** [assumed] **Widening `MoveTarget` must leave the card menu identical.** The id joins the node as an addition, and `buildMoveTargets` moving out of `CardsView` is a relocation rather than a rewrite — the card's `Move To ▸` behaves exactly as it does today, or the change is wrong.
- **J-9:** [confirmed] **The date lane is fixed and the breadcrumb yields to it.** The title keeps its flexible share and the breadcrumb's ceiling comes down, since a breadcrumb already truncates gracefully at either edge while a date that wraps or clips stops being readable at all.

#### L — Context and Space Take Their Own Glyphs

A nexus-wide change the trash surfaced rather than a trash-local one. It travels with this work because the trash is what made the collision visible, but its effects are felt everywhere either kind draws a type-level glyph.

- **L-1:** [confirmed] **Context joins `ENTITY_ICON_KINDS` and seeds `layout-grid`.** The reason is not that Contexts are iconless — it is that the kind argument is a lie. Every surface drawing a Context asks `entityIcon` for a **Space**, and gets the Space seed by accident of the two being alike. Naming the kind honestly is the fix; the seed makes that name mean something.
- **L-2:** [confirmed] **Space's seed changes to `layout-dashboard`.** The two kinds stop sharing a mark, and each says what it is.
- **L-3:** [confirmed] **Six surfaces ask for a Space when they mean a Context.** The sidebar's Context group is the visible case; the breadcrumb crumb, the page properties pane's Context chips, the view pipeline's chip identity and the preview inspector do the same. Fixing only the sidebar splits the Context glyph across surfaces — the rest would follow Space to its new seed while the sidebar showed the grid.
- **L-10:** [confirmed] **The ribbon's Contexts tab resolves through the Context kind** and takes the grid, rather than continuing to wear whatever Spaces wear. It is the sixth borrowing site and the only one visible on the ribbon itself.
- **L-4:** [confirmed] **The minted literal has to move with the seed, or the seed is inert.** Every Context is created carrying `layout-grid` as its **own** stored icon, in both the main-side write and the renderer's optimistic mirror. An own icon outranks a nexus default, so a `defaultIcons.context` key would be ignored for every Context the app has ever made. Either the mint stops stamping a literal and lets the kind seed answer, or the new key is decoration.
- **L-5:** [confirmed] **`layout-dashboard`'s overlap with the Gallery view type is accepted.** The two never appear as peers the way a Context and a Space do, so the mark is not doing identifying work in the same place twice.
- **L-6:** [assumed] **Nothing a user chose is disturbed.** An entity's own icon and a nexus's `defaultIcons` override both outrank the seed, so this moves the floor and never a decision anyone made.
- **L-7:** [assumed] **The personalization schema widens by one key and owes no migration.** `defaultIcons` is keyed by `EntityIconKind`, so admitting `context` admits a new settings key; unknown keys already drop on read, and nothing validates this shape with a schema.
- **L-8:** [confirmed] **A test asserts the current Space seed and breaks with it.** The change carries that test, which section K's documentation sweep does not cover.
- **L-9:** [confirmed] **This is its own commit.** It is a nexus-wide visual change the trash merely made visible — six call sites, two minted literals, a seed, a widened kind union and a test. It rides alongside this work rather than inside it.

#### Sequencing

- The **Context/Space glyph change** (→ L) is its own commit and a **hard prerequisite**, not a sibling free to land later: the trash resolves a Context row's glyph by kind, and that kind does not exist in the icon union until L widens it. Building the trash first is a compile error.
- The **search-input extraction** (→ E-8, E-11) is a design-system simplification with three existing consumers and no dependency on the trash; it is not a prerequisite and does not belong in the feature's own commits.
- The **Copy Link / Copy Path pass** (→ M) is independent of everything here and lands first; the closeout verifies it rather than trusting its report.

#### Prospects (allowed later, not now)

- **Previewing a deleted page** — reading a trashed page's body before deciding. The Page Preview window is tab-neutral and already portable, but it resolves a live tree path; a trash preview reads a file the tree does not contain. Don't-foreclose: keep the row's `bundlePath` addressable rather than collapsing rows to display strings.
- **Sorting** — column sort on any of the three columns. Don't-foreclose: keep the row list an array the leaf owns, not a pre-sorted payload.
- **Deleted properties** — the sixth record kind, whose bundle holds a record and no artifact, and whose restore rebuilds a definition, its Collection assignments, and every recorded value rather than moving a file. It has no title, no path and no breadcrumb, so it cannot wear a row this list's shape admits. Don't-foreclose: filter on the record's own `entity` discriminator rather than on the presence of an artifact.
- **Emptying the whole trash** in one action.
- **A `trashMode` switch** — the per-device nexus-vs-system delete target is live code with no writer and no UI; it is hand-edited JSON today.
- **Pruning** — nothing in `.trash` is ever removed. An age or size policy is a product call nobody has made.

#### Out of Scope (won't do — distinct from Prospects)

- **Restoring from the operating system's trash.** Once a bundle leaves, Pommora has no claim on it; the OS owns the undo.
- **Re-minting a colliding identity.** A live id outranks every other answer and nothing writes over a living identity; the destination submenu answers a missing home, never a duplicate id.
- **Reconstructing a missing parent** — minting the Set or Context an entity used to live in. The destination submenu offers what exists; it never creates.
- **Surfacing incomplete bundles.** A bundle holding a record but no artifact is a deletion that never finished; `listBundles` already skips it, and it is evidence rather than an offer.

#### Considered & Rejected

- **Driving the real `TableView`** — rejected on inspection rather than principle. It takes a container node and resolves schema, view, values and orders from it; the synthetic Collection needed to feed it would be more code than the list it replaces, and would couple the trash to the property system it has nothing to do with.
- **Reusing `{ op: 'delete' }` for permanent delete** — refused by `isReserved` at the write path's own guard. Loosening that guard to admit `.trash` would weaken a deliberate defense for one caller's convenience.
- **Filling the rail's reserved band with an `onScan` glyph** rather than removing it — the opposite fix, and one prop away. Rejected as scope: the Settings window has no full-page form to promote into.

#### Found And Reported

- **An ordinary move does not drop conflicting property values, and the project's rules now say it should.** The rule that a moved page's conflicting properties are dropped to suit its target's schema is honored by the restore path — which reconciles a returning entity against the world it lands in — and not by the move path, which relocates the file and rewrites the destination's order without touching frontmatter. This is outside the trash's scope and is named here rather than left for someone to rediscover.

#### Lessons

- A comment naming what a reservation clears can be wrong about which side of the window it clears — `.settings-rail-list`'s padding blamed the `×`, which sits over the opposite pane.
- "It already exists" and "it is reachable" are different findings. Three separate mechanisms here — `listBundles`, the restore op, and `trashMode: 'system'` — are complete, tested, and unreachable from any surface.
